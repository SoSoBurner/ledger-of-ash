# Soreheim Proper — Text RPG Flavor Packet

**Parent polity:** Soreheim Alliance
**Type:** Macro-capital core — tower-centered production, alliance governance, war output
**Truth layer:** reconciled_canon

## Atmosphere

The Titan Towers are the first thing visible from the approach roads and the last thing visible on departure, and they dominate the city's interior as completely as they dominate its skyline. The towers are not uniform — they range from the original massive core structures to the newer administrative annexes that have grown against their bases — but they all share the same volcanic stone, quarried from the same source, fitted without mortar. The fitting is a point of technical pride in Soreheim that functions simultaneously as a practical boast and a political statement: we built this and we did not need outside assistance to do it.

Industrial output fills the air with a persistent smell of hot metal and rendering stone, and the work shifts run through the night on the tower floors visible from the lower streets. The allocation system — centralized supply distribution run through the Allocation Hall — means there is no open market in Soreheim Proper in the conventional sense. You do not browse. You submit a request, receive a determination, and collect at the designated window. For residents who have grown up with this system it is unremarkable. For outsiders it reads as either efficient or controlled, depending on what they were expecting.

## Scene Openers

1. **Dawn shift transition, Allocation Hall approach.** The overnight crew from the upper tower floors is moving through the main transit corridor as the day shift replaces them. The tower workers are marked by the specific grime of their floor — stone dust on one set of coats, metal shavings on another — and they do not mix socially in the corridor. Shift crews are cohesive inward units. Being identifiably from no crew is a status the corridor reads immediately.
2. **Midmorning, the officer allocation window.** A junior alliance officer is presenting his quarterly supply requisition at the officer-tier window. The allocation clerk is processing it line by line, and on the third line the clerk pauses — the quantity requested exceeds the officer's current rank tier by a margin that requires a secondary signature. The officer did not know this. The clerk is explaining the process calmly, because this is not an unusual misunderstanding.
3. **Afternoon, the tower base transit ring.** A foreman is moving materials between tower bases using the transit ring's internal cart system. He is moving faster than the system is designed for, which means he has either gotten an authorization that lets him skip the standard spacing protocol or he is gambling that the overseers are occupied elsewhere. The workers he passes are watching with the practiced neutrality of people who have learned not to visibly notice administrative risk.
4. **Evening, the Allocation Hall officer's quarter.** Three officers from different tower departments are in the common area. None of them is senior to the others in formal rank, which means the social dynamics in the room are running on informal factors — length of service, quota performance, which tower floor they came up on. A visitor can read the room's hierarchy within a few minutes by watching who defers to whom when a decision needs to be made.

## Social Register

- Tower rank is addressed first and corrected immediately if the address is wrong. Getting someone's rank wrong is not interpreted charitably — the system is legible and ignorance of it reads as either disrespect or unfamiliarity that the speaker should have resolved before arrival.
- Quota fulfillment is public. Production floors post their current period numbers, and workers know each other's output. Falling behind quota in a visible way creates social pressure from peers before it creates administrative pressure from oversight.
- Requests through the Allocation Hall system are formal and logged. Making a request at the wrong window, for the wrong tier, or without correct documentation does not result in help — it results in a redirect and a note. The note is not punitive in isolation. It accumulates.
- The industrial areas around the tower bases are not public access. Moving through them without a work assignment or visitor authorization is a security matter handled by the tower floor supervisors, not the general civil authority. The distinction matters for who you will deal with if stopped.
- Faith in Soreheim Proper runs through the tower oath-cycles — seasonal affirmations of production commitment tied to the tower crews' schedules. These are not optional for workers. For visitors, acknowledging them respectfully is expected. Treating them as quaint is a significant misread.

## Signature Details

- **The volcanic stone without mortar.** The original Titan Towers are dry-fitted — every stone placed with precision sufficient to hold without bonding compound. The technique is taught in the Soreheim craft schools as a foundation skill, and the towers are inspected quarterly for settling. A new crack in an old tower is a public matter discussed by tower workers with the specificity of people whose safety depends on the assessment.
- **The Allocation Hall queue numbers.** The Hall issues numbered tokens at its entrance, and the number sequence determines processing order regardless of who you are. Senior officers and visiting dignitaries use the same tokens as everyone else. The only departure from the token system is the officer-tier windows, which have their own queue, shorter but not faster.
- **The shift-change grime patterns.** Workers from different tower floors carry different material signatures — identifiable by their work coats. A worker from the foundry floors has a different grime than a worker from the stone-fitting crews, different again from the administrative tower floors. Locals read these marks automatically. Wearing a coat with the wrong grime for your stated purpose is noticed.

## Complication Seed

A production quota for one of the mid-tier tower floors is being met exactly — to the unit, to the day, for the last six weeks. The floor overseer who reports this is not unusual in herself, but a quota met exactly and consistently, with no variation, is a statistical pattern that the Alliance's internal monitoring office considers a signal worth examining. Natural production has variance. Perfect consistency implies either a system for managing output that the monitoring office has not been told about, or records being adjusted at the point of submission. The monitoring officer assigned to the preliminary review is a methodical woman who has not yet determined which explanation fits the evidence, and she is not authorized to escalate until she has ruled out the benign possibilities. She is looking for someone outside the tower's social network who can observe the floor without being read as an inspector.
