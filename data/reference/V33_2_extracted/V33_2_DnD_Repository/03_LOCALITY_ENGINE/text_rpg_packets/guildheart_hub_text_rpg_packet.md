# Guildheart Hub — Text RPG Flavor Packet

**Parent polity:** The Union
**Type:** Settlement — industrial-urban nerve center, contract and freight administration
**Truth layer:** conservative_inference

## Atmosphere

The Hub is organized around transaction. Every public space is a place where business is either being done or waiting to be done: the counting halls on the main tier run all day with their ledger tables open to the street, the bonded warehouse blocks on the lower ring have queue markers painted on the stone, and the canal-side loading areas operate on a schedule posted at both ends of the dock in the same format the Union uses for all its freight corridors. The architecture is functional in the way that buildings designed for long-term use tend to be functional — no ornament, but nothing cheap either. The counting halls have been here for decades and they will be here decades more, and the walls know it.

The Union's presence is felt through process. You do not encounter authority directly in Guildheart Hub so much as you encounter the systems authority has built: the queuing protocols, the hearing schedules, the freight manifest format that every carrier submits at the same window in the same order. Deviation from process is not punished immediately. It is noted, and the note accumulates.

## Scene Openers

1. **Dawn, the main counting hall threshold.** A factor from an outside merchant house is trying to establish precedence for his submission in the morning queue. The clerk behind the window is explaining, without hostility, that the precedence system does not work the way the factor believes it works. This conversation has happened before with different people.
2. **Midmorning, the tariff mediation queue.** Two carriers whose freight arrived at the same manifest window within the same quarter-hour are waiting for a hearing officer to determine whose claim takes seniority. They are not speaking to each other. They are both watching the clock on the hall wall, which determines which waiting costs whose time.
3. **Afternoon, bonded warehouse dock.** A foreman is doing a count of a shipment before it goes into bond. He is doing it slowly, because a fast count at this stage is a count the Collective will question later, and a questioned count is a hearing. His two assistants have learned to match his pace.
4. **Evening, the canal-side tavern near the departure dock.** Carriers and factors from three different routes are present. Nobody is discussing their cargo. They are discussing the hearing schedule for next month and which time slots have been assigned to which mediators, because the mediator matters as much as the evidence in some cases.

## Social Register

- Declare business plainly before anything else. The Hub's operating assumption is that everyone present has a legitimate purpose and the system will accommodate it. Failing to state business clearly reads as an attempt to avoid the system, which triggers scrutiny.
- Queue and hearing order are not suggestions. Attempting to compress the process or reach a decision-maker outside the scheduled channel is a formal breach, logged and referenced in subsequent dealings.
- The carriers and factors who use the Hub regularly have a social hierarchy invisible to outsiders — built entirely on record of contract fulfillment. Speaking to a senior factor as an equal before establishing your own record is presumptuous. Letting them assume you are lower-status than you are is tactically useful.
- Disputes brought to the Hub's mediation process are expected to be documented before the hearing, not during it. Arriving without documentation is not a bar to being heard — but it marks the claimant as either unprepared or hoping to shift evidentiary burden to the other party.
- Public faith is present but secondary. The Hub has a small devotional alcove maintained by the carriers' cooperative, used mostly before long routes. Pausing to observe it is appropriate. Lingering near it when others are using it reads as surveillance.

## Signature Details

- **The manifest window format.** Every submission to the Hub uses the same physical format — a specific-width folio with the carrier mark, the consignee, the manifest number, and the freight description in a prescribed order. A submission in the wrong format is not rejected. It is set aside until it is corrected, which means it goes to the bottom of the day's queue.
- **The hearing clock.** The clock on the main counting hall wall runs the day's schedule. It is not decorative — mediators use it to open and close hearings, and a hearing that runs over its allotted block is ended whether complete or not. The unresolved portion is rescheduled, which takes weeks.
- **The bonded warehouse chalk marks.** Each loaded bay gets a chalk mark indicating the day of receipt and the expected release date. Marks that have been rewritten more than once — visible from the fresher chalk on older lines — indicate a shipment in dispute, held beyond its original window.

## Complication Seed

A freight carrier submitted a manifest eleven days ago that should have cleared mediation by now. It has not cleared because the counter-party to the consignment — a merchant house from outside the Union — has not responded to the formal notice sent to their registered address. The Hub's process treats non-response as neither admission nor denial; the case sits in the pending queue consuming a slot. The carrier whose shipment is held in bond cannot release it until the case closes. He has tried the official channel twice and been told, correctly, that the process must complete. He has begun to ask around, quietly, whether anyone knows whether the merchant house's registered address is still active — because if the house has dissolved or relocated without updating their Union registration, the process has a different path, faster and with different documentation requirements. He is looking for someone who can check the address without creating a formal inquiry, because a formal inquiry extends the timeline further.
