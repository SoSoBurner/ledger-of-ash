# Sunspire Haven — Text RPG Flavor Packet

**Parent polity:** Principality of Shelk (frontier administration)
**Type:** Haven settlement — highland refuge, route observation, seasonal shelter
**Truth layer:** conservative_inference

## Atmosphere

The Sunspire is a natural formation — a column of pale limestone that catches morning light above the haven's highest point and produces a visible signal from the approach routes, which is the reason the haven was established here in the first place. The original settlement was a waypoint shelter, and the current settlement is still primarily that, though it has grown enough to have a small permanent population of route wardens, supply factors, and service workers who support the seasonal traffic.

The highland air has a specific quality that visitors from lower elevations notice for the first two days and then forget they noticed. The settlement's structures are built for the weather rather than for appearance — thick-walled, low-profiled, shuttered rather than windowed on the prevailing-wind side — and the permanent residents have the physical adaptations of people who live in altitude and cold: slower moving than necessary on flat ground, more economical with effort than lowlanders tend to be. The spire itself is visible from every point in the settlement and visible from the approach roads at distances that make it a navigation marker at elevation. Local tradition holds that a clear spire morning means a clear day; an obscured spire morning means conditions worth checking before committing to the high routes.

## Scene Openers

1. **Dawn, the spire observation point.** The route warden on overnight duty is completing the morning condition assessment from the highest observation post — checking visibility, cloud pattern, wind at the spire's base, and comparing it against the weather record to determine the day's route assessment. Today is clear enough that the assessment will be favorable, but she is annotating a wind condition that has been building for three days, because patterns matter more than single readings.
2. **Midmorning, the supply depot.** A factor is completing a resupply order for a group of highland travelers who need provisions for the next route segment. The group has underestimated their needs by the standard lowlander margin — they packed for the distance, not the elevation — and the factor is showing them the adjusted calculation without editorializing, because the calculation is the information and the editorial is unnecessary.
3. **Afternoon, the common shelter.** The haven's common space serves travelers at all stages: arriving, resting, preparing to depart. Three groups are present without interacting — a highland family who uses the haven seasonally and knows where everything is, two route wardens on rotation break, and a pair of lowland travelers who found the high route harder than they expected and are deciding whether to continue or turn back. The family can read the lowlanders' exhaustion and has not commented.
4. **Evening, the warden station.** The day-shift route warden is debriefing the overnight warden on current route conditions, including two points on the east approach where the path surface has deteriorated and is being flagged for seasonal maintenance. The debrief is precise and brief, because both wardens have done this many times and the information is what matters.

## Social Register

- The morning spire observation is the de facto route advisory. Travelers who bypass it and ask route wardens directly for conditions are not turned away, but the wardens will reference the morning assessment rather than offering a separate opinion. The morning assessment is the opinion.
- Supply calculations at altitude use a different margin than lowland provisioning. Travelers who argue with the factor's adjusted calculation are listened to once, and then the factor gives them the option to take the adjusted quantity or the quantity they requested. The factor notes which they chose.
- The permanent residents of Sunspire Haven distinguish themselves from seasonal travelers not by how they dress — the weather makes everyone dress similarly — but by how they read the settlement's physical cues. Where to shelter from a particular wind direction, which approach to the supply depot is clear of ice in winter, how to read the spire's shadow in the afternoon to estimate remaining daylight. Locals move with these readings internalized.
- Highland route information — current conditions, hazard points, cache locations — is shared freely among travelers, because the alternative is people dying from preventable mistakes. This generosity is specific to route information and does not extend to other kinds of information.
- The haven's overnight shelter is available to all travelers at no cost. There is a donation point and people who can afford to contribute are expected to, because the haven's permanent staff run on the understanding that most travelers will contribute something and some will contribute nothing.

## Signature Details

- **The spire at dawn.** The limestone column's color at first light shifts from gray to a cream-yellow as direct sunlight hits it, and at the transition moment the column appears to glow from within for perhaps two minutes. Long-term residents do not stop to watch this; it is simply a part of the morning. Visitors who see it for the first time tend to stop and are late for whatever they were planning to do at dawn.
- **The weather record board.** A physical board maintained by the route warden station tracks the morning condition assessment for the past thirty days, with symbols for visibility, wind, and temperature. Travelers who are making route decisions study it carefully. The pattern of the past month tells more than any single assessment.
- **The route cache marks.** At intervals along the highland routes, the warden service maintains supply caches — emergency provisions in weather-resistant containers. The cache locations are known to wardens and marked on the route documentation given to registered travelers. The marks are a specific geometric symbol that is not found on any other signage.

## Complication Seed

One of the highland route caches on the east approach has not been restocked in two monthly cycles. The route warden responsible for that section logged the cache as restocked on both cycles in the formal record, but another warden who used the cache in transit last week found it at partial contents — not empty, but not at the level a full restock would produce. She has not yet brought this to the senior warden because she is uncertain whether what she found represents a missed restock or a heavier-than-usual draw by travelers who legitimately needed the supplies. The distinction matters because one is a record error and one is a sign that the eastern approach is seeing more traffic than the route logs reflect.
