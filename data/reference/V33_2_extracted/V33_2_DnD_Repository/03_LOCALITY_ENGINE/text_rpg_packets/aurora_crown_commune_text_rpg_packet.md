# Aurora Crown Commune — Text RPG Flavor Packet

**Parent polity:** Sheresh Communes
**Type:** Major dome commune — survival administration, celestial study, public endurance ritual
**Truth layer:** conservative_inference

## Atmosphere

The dome is the defining physical fact of the Crown. Blue-white in daylight, it catches the low-angle light of the northern seasons differently than any other structure in the region, and at the right hour it produces a secondary light that washes the inner commune in a color that has no exact equivalent in other places. The layered ice walls at the dome's base are not decorative — they are part of the thermal management system, and their maintenance is a civic responsibility assigned by rotation to working-age commune members. You can tell a resident from a visitor in part by how they look at the ice walls: residents scan for stress fractures as a reflex; visitors tend to look at the color.

The commune runs on a survival logic that is also a social logic: resource management, thermal maintenance, and endurance observation are all public activities because they all affect everyone. The celestial study program — mapping light patterns, tracking seasonal pressure, predicting axial conditions — is not abstract scholarship. It informs when the commune tightens its thermal protocols, when it opens the outer gates, when it brings in the exterior supplies. The scholars who run the program are functional administrators as much as they are researchers, and the commune treats them accordingly.

## Scene Openers

1. **Dawn, the thermal inlet inspection.** The rotating maintenance crew is working the inlet valves on the east section of the dome base. One valve is running warmer than the others — not critically, but enough to mark on the maintenance log and flag for the week's review. The crew leader is deciding whether to mark it as a watch item or an action item, because the classification determines which team handles follow-up and the action-item team has a longer queue.
2. **Midmorning, the celestial observation tower.** A junior scholar is tracking the current light measurement against the seasonal baseline and finding a deviation she cannot explain by the standard factors. She is checking her instrument calibration because that is the first thing you check, and the calibration is correct, which makes the deviation more interesting and more concerning in equal measure.
3. **Afternoon, the public distribution point.** The commune's current-period supply allocation is being distributed at the central point. The distribution is orderly in the way of a system practiced enough to need no explanation — residents know the sequence, the portion assignments, the protocols for disputed allocations. A visitor who doesn't know the sequence slows the line, and the resident behind them will explain it once, without irritation, because once is enough.
4. **Evening, the endurance rite at the dome's inner ring.** A communal gathering at the inner perimeter, attended by a portion of the commune's households — not all are required, but non-attendance is remarked on informally. The rite involves a spoken cycle of acknowledgments — of the year's conditions, of resources consumed, of what was asked and what was given. It is not religious in a doctrinal sense. It is an accounting, performed aloud, because the commune believes the accounting should be shared.

## Social Register

- Maintenance rotation is a civic obligation. Attempting to trade away or defer a rotation assignment without the commune's scheduling body formally approving the trade is an informal breach that the commune tracks through its social memory rather than its formal records.
- The celestial scholars are addressed by their study designation, not by name, in formal contexts. The designation indicates their role in the observation cycle. Treating them as general scholars rather than functional administrators misreads their position in the commune's governance.
- Resource questions — how much is available, when the next supply arrives, what the current thermal load is — are answered directly and without hedging, because the commune operates on the principle that everyone needs accurate information to make correct decisions. Hedging a resource question is interpreted as either ignorance or concealment, both of which concern people.
- The dome's inner areas near the thermal management infrastructure are not restricted but they are not casual public space either. Moving through them with no evident purpose draws attention from the maintenance crews who work there, and they will ask about it directly.
- Endurance rites are commune-wide events. Attending as a visitor is acceptable if done with attention and without commentary during the cycle. Commentary during the cycle — even appreciative commentary — is a significant breach.

## Signature Details

- **The dome's secondary light hour.** At a specific window in the early afternoon, the angle of external light through the dome's panels produces a diffused secondary illumination inside the commune's main public space. Residents do not mark this consciously, but they tend to gather there during that window for tasks that benefit from diffuse light, and the pattern has become social infrastructure without ever being planned.
- **The ice wall stress marks.** Long-term residents can read the ice walls the way they read each other — the maintenance crews have developed a classification system for stress types, and there are informal names for specific recurring patterns that have no official designation. Knowing the informal names places you as someone who has been here long enough to absorb them.
- **The endurance ledger.** The commune keeps a public record — updated after each endurance rite — of the period's resource inputs and outputs, the thermal events, and the celestial readings. It is available at the distribution point. Residents consult it when making decisions about personal resource planning. The ledger is accurate because inaccuracy would be immediately noticed and immediately corrected by the people whose planning depends on it.

## Complication Seed

A celestial measurement from three weeks ago has produced a reading that does not fit the current seasonal model — a light-pattern deviation that, if real, indicates a shift in axial conditions that the commune's thermal protocols were not designed for. The senior scholar who recorded the reading has not published it in the commune ledger because she is not certain whether the reading is real or a calibration artifact, and she needs three consecutive confirming readings to be certain. She has two. The third measurement window opens in eight days. In the meantime, the commune is operating on thermal protocols that may be insufficient for the conditions the reading implies — but adjusting protocols on the basis of an unconfirmed reading has its own costs, and the senior scholar is the one who decides.
