# Shirshal — Text RPG Flavor Packet

**Parent polity:** Shirshal
**Type:** Administrative center — civic registration, polity coordination, transit checkpoint
**Truth layer:** conservative_inference

## Atmosphere

Shirshal runs on paper in the way that some cities run on stone or water — the civic registration systems, the checkpoint documentation, the transit records, and the polity coordination correspondence are all physical, handled in the administrative quarter's long halls where the filing systems are organized by a taxonomy developed over decades and now too embedded in institutional practice to reform. The clerks who work these halls know their sections the way warehouse workers know their bays: by feel as much as by label, moving through the stacks with the efficiency of people who have stopped needing to look for things.

The settlement is not architecturally distinguished. The civic quarter is the most solid construction; the residential areas behind it are comfortable without being prosperous; the market district at the settlement's edge is active without being extensive. What Shirshal has is administrative legibility — if you need to establish your status, register a claim, resolve a documentation question, or locate a record that exists somewhere in the polity's history, Shirshal is the place where that work is done. The people who come here are generally people who have a specific administrative purpose, and the settlement's character has absorbed this over time.

## Scene Openers

1. **Dawn, the registration queue.** Three parties are waiting at the registration hall entrance before it opens. Two of them are seasonal arrivals who need their transit status confirmed for the current period. The third has been here four times in the last month for a registration matter that has not resolved, and the clerk who handles her file knows her face and the file's contents and the reason it has not resolved, which is that the counter-party to her registration claim has not responded to the formal notification. The hall opens. The regular line goes first.
2. **Midmorning, the filing hall.** A clerk is being consulted by a junior colleague on the taxonomy for a new document type — a combined transit-and-registration filing that the polity coordination office started accepting last year without clarifying where in the existing taxonomy it should sit. The senior clerk has a view. The junior clerk is writing it down. The view will become the practice for this hall until someone with more authority contradicts it.
3. **Afternoon, the transit checkpoint at the settlement's approach.** A warden is conducting a document check on a small cargo convoy. The documents are in order. The warden is thorough because a check that is not thorough has no point. The convoy driver is patient because impatience at a checkpoint creates a different kind of attention. Both are performing a function they have performed many times with other parties, and both know it.
4. **Evening, the administrative quarter's common room.** The senior clerks and the registration officers who overlap shift ends use this room to transition between the day's business and the evening. The conversation is partly social and partly a continuation of the day's work — cases that need a second opinion, ambiguities that could go either way, the occasional situation where the senior officer will want to know before she comes in tomorrow. The room is not officially a meeting space. It functions as one.

## Social Register

- Registration claims require the claimant to be present or formally represented. A representative who lacks the correct authorization document is not an error the hall will work around — the representation must be corrected before the claim can be processed.
- The filing taxonomy is institutional knowledge that the clerks hold and visitors do not. Asking a clerk to find a record for you is legitimate; directing a clerk to a specific location in the taxonomy based on your own understanding of it is a presumption that slows rather than speeds the process.
- The transit checkpoint wardens are a distinct authority from the registration hall staff. Documents accepted at one office are not automatically accepted at the other. A traveler who assumes that cleared documentation from one context transfers to the other will be redirected, once, before the warden begins to have questions about the assumption.
- Shirshal's administrative pace is not slow — it is deliberate. The clerks work at the speed that prevents errors, which is not the same as the speed that feels productive to someone who is waiting. Communicating impatience to a clerk produces a colder interaction but the same pace.
- Polity coordination matters — disputes between parties from different adjacent polities, inter-polity registration claims, cross-boundary transit arrangements — are handled in a separate section of the administrative quarter by officers who have specific training for them. Attempting to handle these matters at the general registration window is a misdirection that the window clerk will correct without irritation and without further help.

## Signature Details

- **The file taxonomy markers.** The filing halls use a color and symbol system on the folder ends that codes for document type, period, and polity of origin. The system has evolved additions over decades without being redesigned, which means the original colors are now supplemented by secondary marks, tertiary marks, and a handful of marks whose origin the current clerks cannot fully explain but have found it simpler to maintain.
- **The registration queue number.** Parties waiting at the registration hall are given a numbered card at the entrance. The card determines order of service and is date-stamped by the intake desk. A party that returns the next day must take a new card — the previous day's number does not carry over. This rule has no exceptions and is the first thing explained to parties who arrived the day before.
- **The checkpoint warden's notation.** The transit wardens make a notation on every document they check — a brief record of the check date, the warden's mark, and any flags. The notation is small and placed in a consistent corner of the document. Experienced travelers look for it when they receive their documents back; its absence means the document was not checked at this checkpoint, which has implications for subsequent checkpoints.

## Complication Seed

A registration claim filed eight months ago has been technically complete and technically pending since its third week — all required documentation submitted, counter-party notified, no response. The polity's process for unresolved claims of this type calls for a dormancy review at the six-month mark, which was conducted and produced a recommendation to extend the notification window by another six months. The claimant is a woman who came to Shirshal specifically for this registration and who has been managing the wait from a distance. She received the dormancy review notification and returned to the hall to ask whether there is anything that can be done to accelerate the counter-party response. The senior registration officer she spoke to was precise in her answer: the process provides one mechanism, a formal inquiry to the counter-party's last known registered address, which has already been used twice. The officer did not say that a third inquiry is not possible — only that the process provides for two. The claimant heard the gap in what was not said and has been trying to determine whether someone will fill it.
