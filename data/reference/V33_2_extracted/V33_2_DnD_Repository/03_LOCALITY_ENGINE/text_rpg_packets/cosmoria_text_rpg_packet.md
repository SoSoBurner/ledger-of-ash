# Cosmoria — Text RPG Flavor Packet

**Parent polity:** Cosmouth
**Type:** Coastal trade town — maritime commerce, passage documentation, route brokering
**Truth layer:** conservative_inference

## Atmosphere

Cosmoria sits where three trade corridors meet the sea, and the convergence is visible in everything the town does. The dockside runs longer than the settlement's population would suggest necessary, because the docks are not for local use — they are for transit, for the freight moving between inland routes and the coastal shipping lanes. The warehousing quarter above the docks is dense and operates at hours that the rest of the town does not, because freight arrives when it arrives, and the receiving clerks keep the same schedule as the tide tables.

The town's architecture has the layered quality of a place that has been continuously in use rather than continuously planned. The oldest buildings are stone, built low and wide against the coastal weather. The newer structures climb the hill behind them in lighter materials, and the mix gives Cosmoria a quality of geological strata — you can read how old a district is by how high it sits and what it is built from. The oldest stone buildings at waterline are the ones with the most complicated histories, and the people who work in them have the longest institutional memories.

## Scene Openers

1. **Dawn, the receiving dock.** A night arrival is being processed — a coastal freight vessel whose manifest is being checked by the dock clerk against the corresponding inland receiving order. One line in the manifest does not correspond. The discrepancy is small enough to be an error and large enough to be something else, and the clerk is deciding whether to flag it for the senior receiver or handle it as a routine correction.
2. **Midmorning, the passage documentation office.** A traveler seeking transit papers for a southern coastal route is learning that the route's documentation requirements changed two months ago and his paperwork is for the previous format. The clerk who tells him this is sympathetic but cannot process the old format. The traveler now needs to determine whether he can get the correct format here, or whether he needs to return to the issuing office.
3. **Afternoon, the route broker's house.** A broker is negotiating simultaneous passage arrangements for three separate parties who all need to move on the same route in the same window. She is keeping the three parties from knowing about each other while she determines which arrangement is most profitable to broker, which is a standard part of her practice and which all three parties would consider manipulative if they knew.
4. **Evening, the waterline tavern.** Sailors, clerks, and inland carriers share the same public space for the first and usually only time in their daily schedule. The social mixing is practical — passage arrangements get made over food because the alternative is formal office hours during the day. The tavern keeper maintains studied neutrality between all parties and remembers everything.

## Social Register

- Manifest discrepancies are handled through the receiving office, not the dock itself. Attempting to resolve a discrepancy directly with the carrier at the dock rather than through the clerk is a breach of the documentation system's authority, and the dock clerk will redirect rather than engage.
- Route brokers hold competitive information that is legally theirs to keep. Asking a broker which other parties are moving on a given route is a question that will be answered with nothing. Offering to pay for the information is an insult to the broker's licensing relationship with the passage authority.
- The old stone buildings at waterline have custodial relationships that predate the current documentation system. The people who work in them are addressed by title of role, not name, in the first instance, until they offer something else.
- Freight that has cleared receiving has different standing than freight still in the documentation process. Discussing cleared freight in terms of its route or recipient is acceptable casual conversation. Discussing in-process freight the same way is an informal breach that will be noted by whoever hears it.
- The tide table at the dock entrance is a public document. Reading it is unremarkable. Copying it is not prohibited but is observed by the dock staff, who will form a view of what you intend to do with the information.

## Signature Details

- **The layered stone of the waterline buildings.** The oldest structures at Cosmoria's waterline are built in a fitted stone technique that uses no mortar — a construction method predating the current trade era. The gaps between stones have been maintained over decades, because the fittings shift with seasonal temperature changes and a sealed gap would crack. The maintenance is handled by a family of specialists who have done it for three generations.
- **The manifest format.** Cosmoria's receiving office uses a specific manifest format that differs from the inland route format by three fields — additional columns for coastal handling conditions, tide window, and the receiving party's coastal standing. Inland carriers frequently arrive with the wrong format and frequently need to have it reprocessed at the clerk's window.
- **The broker's schedule board.** The passage brokers' association maintains a board in the market square showing current route availability and estimated passage costs. The board is accurate for open slots and deliberately incomplete for routes with active negotiations. Reading the gaps in the board is as informative as reading the entries.

## Complication Seed

A sealed shipment came through the receiving dock eleven days ago on a coastal freight manifest that cleared documentation without incident. The seal broke during transfer to the inland warehouse — not by tampering, by ordinary handling stress — and the warehouse staff who recorded the damage noticed that the contents did not match the manifest description. The discrepancy is significant enough that the senior receiver has opened a formal inquiry, which means the shipment is now held pending resolution. The original consignee has not been notified because the inquiry requires the receiving office to determine first whether the discrepancy is a documentation error or a substitution. The broker who arranged the passage is not cooperating — she has stated, correctly, that her obligation ended at the receiving dock — and the carrier who transported the freight has left port.
