# Shelkopolis — Text RPG Flavor Packet

**Parent polity:** Principality of Shelk
**Type:** City — major metropolis, fashion and trade center
**Truth layer:** direct_canon

## Atmosphere

Shelkopolis announces itself by scale. Pale stone walls rise above the meadows at an angle that makes the city look taller than it is, and the gold filigree worked into the gate cornices catches morning light before anything inside does. The streets descend in irregular rings from the upper residential tiers to the market levels, and cloth is everywhere — bolts stacked in half-open doorways, sample swatches hung on iron rings above stall entrances, draped lengths moving through the press of the morning crowd on shoulders and over arms. The city's dominant economy is visible at a glance. You cannot walk a block without reading three different fashion houses' marks.

The plurality of devotional sites is a second visible layer. Small shrines occupy corners that in other cities would hold a water barrel or a bench. They are not neglected and they are not competing — the people who light offerings at one may pass the next without pausing, or stop at both. The city's faith runs wide rather than deep, and the Cathedral of the Aurora Light on the upper tier is both devotional center and civic architecture. The building is not a monument to belief. It is a statement of civic confidence.

## Scene Openers

1. **Dawn, the Amber Fountain common room.** Three cloth merchants are seated at the long table comparing samples against a written description — not fabric they're buying, but a contested commission where the original specification may have been falsified. The innkeeper knows the argument by now and has stopped refilling their cups until the tone drops.
2. **Morning, the Fashion Artisans Collective stall block.** A junior factor is posting that day's commission queue on the board outside the Collective hall. Three apprentices from different houses are reading it over his shoulder before he finishes. The order of names on the board determines whose work gets premium placement in the market window, and everyone present understands that the order is not neutral.
3. **Midday, the civic record office.** A scribe is processing a disputed manifest line by line while the merchant who filed it waits in the chair across the ledger table. The scribe's handwriting is very small and very precise. She has not looked up in fifteen minutes. The merchant has begun to understand this is a technique.
4. **Evening, the Cathedral of Aurora Light.** The attendant is sweeping the offering alcoves with the quiet efficiency of someone who has done it ten thousand times. Three worshippers are present in different parts of the nave, none acknowledging the others. The attendant has a sense of who comes on which day and why, and she notes you are not one of the regulars.

## Social Register

- Fashion marks are read before faces. Walking in with a house-marked garment is a statement of affiliation whether you intend it or not. Walking in with no marks at all reads as deliberate neutral status — acceptable but conspicuous.
- The Collective handles disputes through a formal hearing process. Taking a disagreement outside the Collective process before going through it is considered both rude and suspicious, like skipping the official channel to reach a faster unofficial one.
- Asking a scribe to re-read a document is acceptable. Questioning her count in front of witnesses is not — it implies she made an error, which the room hears as an accusation.
- The Cathedral of Aurora Light serves multiple devotional traditions. Treating any one of them as the correct one in the presence of others is a social miscalculation that marks the speaker as provincial.
- Commissioned work is a matter of public record. Asking about a commission you are not party to is presumptuous. Asking about a commission you are party to is expected. The difference matters to the people in the room.

## Signature Details

- **The cloth samples at every threshold.** Merchants and factors leave samples in public-facing hooks and rings outside workshops and stalls — not to advertise, exactly, but to create a record of what was available. Walking the market level in morning light is a way of reading the city's current economic posture without asking anyone directly.
- **The scribe's small handwriting.** Every civic office in Shelkopolis uses a standard record format, but the senior scribes have developed personal notation systems that compress information into a format legible only to trained readers. A document in standard format means routine business. A document with scribe's notation means something was flagged.
- **The aurora-light offering pattern.** The small shrines in the city's lanes and corners accumulate offerings throughout the day — coins, cloth scraps, dried flowers. By evening the pattern of which shrines are full and which are empty tells a practiced reader something about which districts are feeling exposed and which are feeling settled.

## Complication Seed

A commission dispute has been running through the Collective's formal hearing process for eleven days. The disputed item is a bolt of enchanted cloth, the claim is that its properties were misrepresented at sale, and the counter-claim is that the buyer's use of the cloth voided the original properties through negligence. Both parties are represented by factors rather than appearing themselves, which means the dispute is about standing, not fabric. The senior Collective arbitrator is a woman who has handled disputes like this for twenty years, and she is beginning to suspect neither factor is authorized to settle. If she's right, the hearing will be suspended rather than resolved — and a suspended Collective hearing leaves the disputed item in a legal gray zone that someone with the right access could exploit before the parties regroup.
